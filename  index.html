The mic still sees ~46.8 V at peak. You'd need a run near 328 feet (100 meters) before resistance alone starts to matter, and even then the problem would be time-of-flight packet collisions, not a brownout.

328 FEET / 100 METERS

That's the Ethernet spec limit for a single segment. Past that, round-trip time exceeds what the protocol allows — frames collide and you get timeouts. It's not that the signal dies. It's that the timing math breaks. With CCA you'd probably have a bad day before you even hit 328 feet because resistance starts choking real current. A 90 W PTZ camera through 328 feet of 24 AWG CCA would see over 60 V of drop from a 48 V source. That's not a brownout. That's a funeral. The GS305GP's total PoE budget is 63 W. That camera wouldn't even power up.

WHAT A BROWNOUT ACTUALLY IS

Voltage sags below spec but doesn't hit zero. The device doesn't shut off cleanly — it gets confused. It reboots randomly, drops packets, forgets its IP, starts acting possessed. That's what the mic does every morning. It doesn't die. It gets stupid. A resistive fault doesn't have a clock. If CCA were starving the mic, it would die mid-sentence during a meeting. It dies at 3 AM when nobody's in the building. That's not a wire. That's a discovery socket going cold.

CCA is also not legal for commercial Category cabling (TIA-568 / NEC). It doesn't meet impedance, resistance, or fire-rating. You've only ever pulled plenum from real distributors. Travis pulled it off Amazon.
