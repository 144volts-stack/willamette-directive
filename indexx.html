MR. HOOT'S COMMON CORE VOLTAGE DROP

Today we find the voltage drop. First we must understand what a wire is.
Step 1: What is a foot?
A foot is twelve inches. Travis's cable goes thirty feet to the ceiling and thirty feet back. That is a round trip. Draw a number line. Start at zero. Hop thirty. Hop thirty more. You are at sixty. Sixty is the whole.
Step 2: Number Bond
Sixty is made of thirty and thirty. 30 + 30 = 60. The parts bond to make the whole. Electricity goes out on one part and comes back on the other. They are friends.
Step 3: Make a Ten (with ohms)
24 gauge CCA is 0.084 ohms per foot. Sixty feet. Group the tens: 10 × 0.084 = 0.84. Six groups. 0.84 × 6 = 5.04 ohms. Give yourself a sticker. The sticker is optional. Some children are allergic to sticker glue.
Step 4: Tape Diagram
Draw a long bar. Blue for electricity going up. Red for coming down. Same length. Symmetry. Nature loves symmetry. Travis does not.
Step 5: The Current
5.7 watts ÷ 48 volts = 0.119 amps. Smaller than a goldfish. If you had 0.119 goldfish you would not have a goldfish. You would have a thought about a goldfish.
Step 6: Multiply
5.04 × 0.119 = 0.60 volts. Less than one volt. On a 48-volt system that is like losing one goldfish out of eighty.
328 feet is 100 meters. Past that, packets run into each other. Time of flight. With CCA at 24 gauge you have a bad day around 320 feet. A 90 W PTZ at 48 V draws 1.9 A. Through 328 ft of CCA that is roughly 32 ohms. Over 60 volts of drop. Negative voltage. That is not a brownout. That is a funeral.
A brownout is when voltage sags but does not hit zero. The device gets confused. It reboots. It drops packets. It forgets its IP. It acts possessed. That is what the mic does at 3 AM. A resistive fault does not have a clock. If the wire were starving the mic it would die mid-sentence. It does not.
